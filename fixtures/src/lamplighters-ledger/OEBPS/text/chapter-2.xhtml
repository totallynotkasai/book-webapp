Chapter 2 The Ledger

Mister Cobb’s front room smelled of lamp oil, peppermint and old paper. Every flat surface held something: coils of wick, tins of brass polish, a broken barometer, a jar of buttons that had outlived their coats. The grey cat walked in as if it had paid rent for years, chose the warmest chair, and went to sleep.

“That’s Wick,” said Mister Cobb.

“You know this cat?”

“Everyone on the canal knows that cat. He has never once followed anybody home.” Mister Cobb lowered himself onto a stool with a noise like a door that needed oiling. “Sit. Tell me about the steps.”

Tam told him. She told him about the grey blanket at the bottom of the steps and the silence where the water should have been, about the smudge that rubbed the colour off the stone, and about the match. While she talked, Mister Cobb listened with his eyes closed. When she reached the part about the window, Weakness: Light that is remembered, he opened them.

“So it’s back,” he said. “I hoped I’d be gone before it came back.”

“You’ve seen it before?”

“Once. When I was younger than you are now. My master put me to bed and told me to stay there, and in the morning half the old town had forgotten its own name for a week.” He pointed at a shelf above the fireplace. “Fetch that down for me. The big one. Careful, it’s heavier than it looks.”

It was a book. It was the largest book Tam had ever touched, bound in brown leather gone soft as a glove, with brass corners and a brass clasp shaped like a little flame. When she lifted it, the window opened by itself.

ITEM

Name: The Lamplighter’s Ledger

Rarity: Unique

Binding: Bound to the Wickery Round. Transfer requires the consent of the current keeper.

Current keeper: Ezra Cobb

Description: Every lamp in this book has been lit by someone who wrote it down.

“Open it,” said Mister Cobb.

The pages were thin and crowded. Each line held a date, a lamp number and a name, written in many different hands and many different inks. The oldest pages were brown and the writing on them had faded to the colour of weak tea. 14th of Frostmonth, lamp 1, lit by Agnes Wray. 14th of Frostmonth, lamp 2, lit by Agnes Wray. Hundreds of lamps. Thousands of nights. Near the back, in the newest ink, the same name over and over: Ezra Cobb, Ezra Cobb, Ezra Cobb, all the way down to the night before last, when the handwriting had gone wobbly.

“Every lamp on the Round, every night, for three hundred and twelve years,” said Mister Cobb. “My master told me it was tradition. I believed him. I think now it was something more than that.”

“Light that is remembered,” Tam said slowly.

“Just so.”

He held out his hand, and she gave him the ledger, and he laid it on his knees and smoothed the last page with his palm. Then he did something Tam did not expect. He took a pencil from behind his ear and wrote, in his careful, shaky hand, at the bottom of the page: Keeper of this ledger from tonight, Tamsin Reed.

[System] Ezra Cobb has transferred The Lamplighter’s Ledger to you. Do you accept? [Yes] [No]

“Mister Cobb, I can’t. It’s yours. I’ve only been a lamplighter for about three hours.”

“And I’ve been one for fifty years, and my knees are telling me that’s plenty.” His eyes crinkled. “Say yes, Tam. It wants you to. It doesn’t open for just anybody, you know. When the Guild came round last spring with their clipboards and their questions, it wouldn’t unclasp for them at all.”

Tam said yes.

The ledger grew warm in her lap, like a cat settling. The window flickered, and the locked line at the bottom of her skills went from grey to gold.

SKILL UNLOCKED

Inscribe (Level 1): Write a lamp you have lit into the Ledger. An inscribed lamp resists the Murk.

Cost: 1 Light and a moment of attention.

Note: The Ledger remembers. So should you.

[System] Level up! You are now Level 2. Light 4 / 4. You gained 1 stat point.

“It says I have a stat point,” Tam said.

“In my day we didn’t have points,” said Mister Cobb. “We had sore feet.” But he leaned forward to look at the window anyway, which he could not see, and nodded seriously when she said she would put it into Light.

There was a knock at the door. Not the lamplighters’ knock: three quick raps, then a pause, then a fourth that sounded more like somebody kicking it by accident.

“That’ll be the Ollery boy,” said Mister Cobb, without getting up. “He brings me yesterday’s bread on his way home. Let him in before he drops it.”

Bram Ollery was a year older than Tam, a head taller and almost entirely covered in flour. He had a paper bag in one hand and his own window hovering, faintly visible, above the other; he had been Named in the summer, and he still poked at it when he thought nobody was looking.

“Evening, Mister Cobb. Evening, Tam.” He blinked at her. “Hang on. Is it your Naming Day? Did you get Named? What did you get?”

“Lamplighter,” said Tam, and waited for him to laugh.

He didn’t. He said, “Oh, that’s brilliant. That’s a real one,” and put the bread down so he could shake her hand, which left flour on it. “I got Baker. Obviously. Everyone in my family gets Baker. Look.”

He flicked his own window towards her. To Tam’s surprise, she could read it; the System apparently allowed that if you meant it to.

“Proof makes things rise,” Tam read.

“Bread, mostly,” Bram admitted. “I tried it on my little brother once. Nothing happened, and Mum shouted at me.” His face changed. “Here, Tam. Did you come along the canal tonight? Only, Dad sent our Pim down to Saltgate with the late order for the Ferry Inn, and he’s not come back, and Dad says the bridge has gone foggy. He said he’d give it an hour.”

Tam looked at Mister Cobb. Mister Cobb looked at the ledger.

“How long ago?” asked Tam.

“About an hour,” said Bram.

Before she had quite decided to, Tam was on her feet with the ledger under one arm and her pole in her other hand. Wick the cat opened one eye, stretched every one of his legs in turn, and dropped off the chair.

“Tam,” said Mister Cobb, and she stopped in the doorway. He was holding out his own lamplighter’s lantern, the small brass one with the dented lid that he had carried on the Round for fifty years. “The Murk eats light it doesn’t know. It can’t eat light that’s been written down. Light your way, and write it as you go. Every lamp. Don’t skip one, however much of a hurry you’re in.”

“Every lamp,” Tam promised.

[System] Quest offered: Find Pim Ollery. Party available: Bram Ollery (Baker, Level 4). Form a party? [Yes] [No]

“Yes,” said Tam, and Bram, who had seen his own copy of the message, said “Yes!” at exactly the same moment, and they both ran out into the dark.
