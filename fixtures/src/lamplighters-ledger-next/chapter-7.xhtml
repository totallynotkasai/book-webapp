Chapter 7 The Night Round

Tam slept for fourteen hours, and when she woke the ledger was sitting on the end of her bed like a cat that wanted its breakfast.

Wick, who was the actual cat, did not think much of this. He sat on the windowsill with his back to both of them and his tail wrapped round his feet, watching the lamplit street below as if he had personally arranged it.

She looked at the third line for a long time. Then she closed the box without choosing anything, because Mister Cobb had always said that a lamplighter who hurried was a lamplighter who burned her fingers.

Downstairs, the bakery was full. It seemed that half of Hollowmere had decided, overnight, that the Ollery oven was lucky, and they had come to buy bread from it while it was still warm from saving the city. Bram was behind the counter in a clean apron with flour up to his elbows, looking as if he could not decide whether to be proud or embarrassed, and settling on both.

“There’s a letter for you,” he said, and slid it across the counter between two loaves. “Guild seal. Came at dawn.”

The wax was grey, stamped with the Lumen Guild’s little sun. Inside, in a hand as straight as a ruler, Guildmaster Vell had written four lines.

Apprentice Reed. The Wickery Round is yours from tonight, and the Old Town Round besides, until we can train more hands. Report at dusk. Bring the ledger. And bring the baker; he appears to be necessary.

“I appear to be necessary,” said Bram, reading it upside down. He went pink. “Mum will have it framed.”

At dusk they walked the Wickery Round together for the first time as a proper crew: Tam with the ledger and the long brass pole, Bram with a basket of rolls for anyone who looked cold, and Wick ranging ahead from post to post with his tail in the air like a small black flag. Every lamp they came to was already burning. Tam touched each one anyway and wrote it down, because that was the job, and because the ledger hummed a little every time she did.

[System] Night Round complete: 64 / 64 lamps. The Round remembers you.

It was on the way home, on Saltgate Bridge, that they saw it. Far out past the harbour wall, where the sea met the dark, a single light was burning on the water. It was not gold. It was a cold, clear blue, and it did not flicker at all.

The ledger in Tam’s arms went very still.

Then, in pale gold ink that wrote itself across a fresh page while she watched, a new line appeared: Lamp unknown. Not lit from Lamp 0.
